# Who to write to

**⚠ No email addresses in this file, on purpose.** I will not invent a contact
address — a wrong one either bounces or, worse, reaches someone it shouldn't
and burns the institution. What follows is *who* to approach and *how to find
the right person*. Verify every address on the institution's own site before
sending.

Confidence is marked so you know what to double-check:
**[verified]** — confirmed in a live search on 2026-09-11, career/employability
function exists. **[known]** — a major institution I'm confident exists, but
whose career-services setup I did not confirm; check before writing.

---

## Why this list is what it is

The search found that Saudi career centres already **run CV-writing workshops
and offer career services online** — [UBT's Career Center](https://ubt.edu.sa/about/career)
explicitly instructs students in CV preparation and runs workshops on CV
writing. That is the buying signal, and it is why career centres come first:
you are not introducing a new idea, you are handing them a better tool for
something they already do every term.

## Tier 4 — recruitment agencies

A different pitch: they don't care about students, they care about turning a
candidate's messy CV into a presentable one fast, in both languages. Worth
trying **only after** you have learned what actually lands from Tiers 1–2.

## How to find the right person

Do this per institution; it takes about five minutes.

1. **Find the function on their own site.** Search the site for *Career
   Center*, *Career Services*, *Employability*, *Alumni Affairs*, or in Arabic
   **مركز التوظيف**, **التوظيف والخريجين**, **الإرشاد المهني**. It usually
   sits under Student Affairs or a Deanship.
2. **Get a name, not a department.** `info@` is a black hole. A director or
   coordinator with a name answers. LinkedIn works well here: search the
   institution name plus *career services* or *الإرشاد المهني*.
3. **Take the address from the site**, not from a guess at the format. If only
   a general address exists, use it but address the email to the named person.
4. **While you're there, grab your one personal line.** A workshop they ran, a
   career fair, a programme they list. It goes in the bracket in the email and
   is the only part that proves you are not mail-merging.
5. **Log it in `05-tracker.csv` before you send.**

## What I could not do

- **No contact addresses**, for the reason at the top.
- **[known] entries are unverified** as to whether they have a career centre
  with an obvious contact route. Check before writing.
- **My search tooling is US-region**, which under-samples Arabic-language and
  Gulf-local results — the same limitation `MONETIZATION.md` §12 records. This
  list is a strong starting set, not a complete census. There are almost
  certainly good targets it did not surface, particularly smaller private
  colleges and technical institutes.
- **I have not checked whether any of these already has a CV-tool partnership.**
