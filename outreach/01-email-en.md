# Cold email + follow-ups — English

Replace `[LINK]`, `[YOUR NAME]`, and the **one** bracketed personal line.

## Email 1 — the first contact

**Subject:** `Arabic CV tool for your students — free, no sign-up`

Alternatives if that one underperforms:
- `A CV builder that gets Arabic right`
- `For your CV workshops — Arabic and English`

> Dear [Name / Career Services team],
>
> [ONE PERSONAL LINE — see `04-targets.md`. E.g. "I saw that your centre runs
> CV-writing workshops for final-year students."]
>
> I built a CV builder that produces the same CV in **Arabic and English**.
> Most free tools accept Arabic text but still lay the page out left-to-right,
> so the result looks wrong to an Arabic reader. This one lays out
> right-to-left properly, and exports a **Word file** that opens correctly in
> Word — which matters because employers here usually ask for an editable
> document, not a PDF.
>
> I've attached one CV exported both ways so you can see it rather than take
> my word for it.
>
> It is free for students: 49 designs, unlimited PDF downloads, no watermark,
> no sign-up, and the CV never leaves the student's browser.
>
> [LINK]
>
> If it would be useful in your next workshop, I'm happy to help you set it up
> — and if there's something it does badly for your students, I'd genuinely
> like to know.
>
> Kind regards,
> [YOUR NAME]

**Why it is shaped this way**

- **The specific claim comes before the offer.** "Arabic that is actually
  right-to-left, and a Word file that opens" is checkable in thirty seconds
  with the attachment. Generic claims ("professional, modern, easy") are what
  every other email in their inbox says.
- **"Free for students" is the whole offer.** No price, no tiers, no meeting.
  The ask is tiny, so the reply is cheap.
- **The last line invites criticism.** People who will not answer a sales email
  will answer a request for their opinion — and if they tell you it fails for
  their students, that is worth more than a yes.
- **No AI, no user numbers, no competitor named.** Every sentence survives
  being checked.
- **About 150 words.** A career services officer reads it on a phone.

## Email 2 — day 7, if no reply

**Reply in the same thread. Do not start a new one.**

> Dear [Name],
>
> Following up briefly on the CV builder I sent last week — the Arabic/English
> one, free for students.
>
> The one thing I'd most like to know: does the Arabic output look right to
> you? That's the part every other free tool gets wrong, and you see more
> student CVs than I ever will.
>
> [LINK]
>
> Kind regards,
> [YOUR NAME]

Shorter, and the ask shrinks from "use this" to "tell me if it's any good."
That is much easier to say yes to, and a person who answers it has started a
conversation.

## Email 3 — day 21, last one

> Dear [Name],
>
> Last note from me — I don't want to clutter your inbox.
>
> The CV builder is at [LINK] and will stay free for students. If it's ever
> useful for a workshop or you'd like Arabic samples for your own materials,
> my door is open.
>
> Thank you for your time.
>
> [YOUR NAME]

**Then stop.** Three emails is persistence; four is a nuisance, and this is a
small professional community. Mark them `no reply` in the tracker and move on
— a door left open gets used months later.

## If they reply

**Answer within a day, in the language they wrote in.** Do not attach a deck
or propose a 30-minute call. Send whatever specific thing they asked about,
and one question back.

`03-objections.md` has the answers. Look them up — do not improvise.
