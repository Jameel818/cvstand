# The questions they will ask, answered in advance

**This file exists so you never have to improvise.** Look the answer up, adapt
the wording, send it. Every answer below is true as of 2026-09-11 — if
something changes in the product, change it here the same day.

Answers are given in English; send them in whichever language they wrote in.

---

### "What does it cost us?"

> Nothing. It's free for your students — all 49 designs, unlimited PDF, no
> watermark, no sign-up. If you later want something specific for your
> institution — your branding on the templates, a workshop version, bulk
> support — that's a separate conversation and I'd quote it then.

**Do not invent a price.** `MONETIZATION.md` §8 says institutional pricing is
"quote", i.e. undecided. "I'd quote it based on what you need" is an honest
answer that keeps the conversation open. A number you made up cannot be
walked back.

---

### "Is our students' data safe? Where is it stored?"

> The CV never reaches my server. It lives in the student's own browser, and
> the server only renders the file and sends it straight back. There's nothing
> to breach because there's nothing stored.

This is true by construction, not a policy promise — the deployment runs with
the server-side store switched off. It is also the strongest single answer in
this file, because it is exactly what a university's IT or legal office worries
about and almost nobody else can say it.

**Do not extend it beyond what is true.** No claims about certifications,
audits, or compliance standards you have not been through.

---

### "Do students need to create an account?"

> No. They open the site and start typing. Nothing to sign up for, no email
> collected, no password to forget.

---

### "Does it work on phones?"

> Yes, the builder works on a phone. The CV itself is a fixed A4-style page,
> so it's more comfortable to edit on a laptop — but nothing is blocked on
> mobile.

Be accurate here rather than enthusiastic; they may well test it.

---

### "Can we put our logo / our branding on it?"

> Not today — that's a real request and one I'd build if a few institutions
> wanted it. What would you need it to look like?

**Turning a no into a question is the point.** Institutional branding is a
plausible paid feature, and this is how you find out whether anyone actually
wants it before building it.

---

### "How is this different from Canva / the free Arabic CV sites?"

> Two things. Canva accepts Arabic text but doesn't lay the page out
> right-to-left — you have to reposition every element by hand. And every free
> Arabic site I found gives you a PDF only; none produce a Word file that
> opens correctly in Arabic. Employers here usually ask for Word.

**State what yours does.** Do not call a competitor bad by name — it reads as
insecurity, and the person reading may use that tool.

---

### "Who are you? Is this a company?"

Answer plainly and briefly. If you are one person who built it, say so — for a
career centre being offered something free, that is not a weakness. Do not
imply a team, an office or funding that does not exist. The single worst
outcome in this whole kit is being caught in a small exaggeration.

---

### "Can you come and present to us?" / "Can we have a call?"

> Happily. Before that, would it help if I set up a sample CV for one of your
> programmes so we have something concrete to look at?

You asked whether outreach could be automated — this is the point where it
can't, and it is also the point where you have already won. They asked. Send
something concrete in advance so the call is about a thing on the screen and
not about you performing.

---

### "Does it use AI?"

> There's an AI writing assistant planned but it isn't available to users yet.
> I'm not shipping it until I'm satisfied the Arabic it produces reads as
> written rather than translated.

**Do not claim the AI works.** It has never made a live call and its Arabic is
unreviewed (`AI_ASSISTANT_PLAN.md` Phase 2). This answer is also a small
credibility win — "I'm not shipping it until it's good" is a better signal
than a feature list.

---

### "We already have a CV tool / a partnership."

> Understood. If it's ever useful as a second option — particularly for
> students who need an Arabic version — the link will stay live and free.

Leave, politely, immediately. Do not argue. They may switch in a year, and a
graceful exit is why they would come back to you.

---

### "Send me more information."

Often a polite brush-off, occasionally real. Treat it as real, and make it
cheap:

> Of course — here's a CV exported in Arabic and in English so you can see the
> output. Is there a particular programme or student group you had in mind?

Attach files, not a brochure. The question at the end is what separates a real
lead from a polite no.

---

### Something you cannot answer

> Good question — I don't want to guess at it. Let me check and come back to
> you today.

Then do come back the same day. "I'll check" costs you nothing; a confident
wrong answer to a university costs you the relationship.
